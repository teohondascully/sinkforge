## A2. T0.2 — the generator moved

**The gap was structural, not a degree of wrongness.** `FactorySim` has carried a complete lode system for
two strikes — `take_lode`, the Drill Head, Spur chaining, the drain fraction, the through-rock stain — and

vein. **Lode was derived from destroying an ore block and never generated.** A fresh world held exactly
zero; the Borer and Drift Rig cut rock with nothing behind it.

**Why nobody could see it: every lode fixture in the suite INJECTS.** `world_seeder` puts lode straight
into the sim, so the seeded path was green for months while the generated path did not exist, and **no
assertion in the project could tell the two apart**. That is now a standing rule in `PRIORITY.md` at the
director's instruction: a controlled lode is sufficient for the blind freight experiment and is **not**
evidence about generated-world behaviour. They fail differently.

**MEASURED — generated world, seed 1337, `LayeredWorldGen`, not a fixture.** 378 lode cells. Determinism
holds both ways (same seed identical, different seed different). All six overlap guards clean at zero:
no lode on an ore-like block (the double-source guard — mining an ore block writes its own lode there and
would overwrite the richness), none off host rock, none without a wall, none in the near-surface shell,
none inside an aquifer, none with a zero deposit. And the chain end to end: **buried → not workable; clear
the rock → workable; work it → yields its ore.** That last sequence is the T0.2 claim and nothing in the
suite could make it before.

**Design calls, flagged as reversible:**

- **Additive, not the cutover.** Phase 3 proper converts the ore blocks themselves, deletes the solid-ore
  path and rewrites the tutorial ladder in one commit. The directive scoped T0.2 to the gen→sim channel,
  so nothing above was touched: the existing economy, every richness assertion and every ore fixture keep
  their exact meaning. *Falsified by:* wanting the ore blocks gone, which is a separate commit.
- **Host rock stays solid.** `_grow_lode` is `_grow_vein`'s blob with one difference — it writes
  `world.lodes` and never `world.blocks`. An ore block is something you destroy; a lode is something you
  uncover.
- **The pass runs DEAD LAST in `generate`.** Every guard tests the final world, and two later passes can
  falsify a guard retroactively: the seal overwrites blocks wholesale, the aquifers carve rock away and
  flood it. Seeding earlier would allow a vein buried inside the unmineable seal, or one hanging in a
  flooded void.
- **`load_world` ingests faithfully**, bounds-checked only. Silently dropping an illegal lode would turn a
  generator bug into missing ore nobody can see; it fails in a test instead.

**ASSUMED — the through-rock tell will change pixels, and I have not diffed captures.** The renderer

## A18b. The `try_*` pacing class — and the discriminator, which needed sharpening once it met a real layer

**THE CLASS (director 0026 confirms it, second instance).** Every `MainView.try_*` verb is SPATIALLY gated
— reach, LOS, solidity, tier, bit. **None of them owns TEMPORAL legality or UI modality.** Those live only
in `_update_mining` and `_unhandled_input`. `main.gd:1706` states it outright: *"Cooldown is input pacing,
not part of the verb."* `try_scan` is the sole exception (it holds its own `_scan_cooldown`) and therefore
the only verb a fixture cannot silently accelerate.

Two confirmed instances, both of which reported numbers the project believed:
- `_scratch_t10_deadhead` — looped `try_work_lode`, bypassing `LODE_CYCLE`. Struck (0023/0024).
- `check_frametime.gd:490` — `_main.try_mine(target)` once per frame, `_update_mining` never called in the
  file. The **"work-proven 32–35 ms"** DIG baseline is struck (0026), with no replacement.

**THE 32–35 ms NUMBER DESERVES ITS OWN NOTE.** It was trusted *because the greener figures had already been
withdrawn* — it read as the conservative survivor of a cleanup. It had the same defect as the numbers it
replaced and **inherited their credibility by outliving them.** A withdrawal that leaves one number
standing implies that one was checked.

### The discriminator I first wrote was too broad, and a peer's finding is what exposed it

`c1` applied my rule and reported `check_dig_hitch` as a **second compromised timing layer** — "drives
`try_mine` in a probe loop, and that layer's whole subject is FRICTION". **I verified it before propagating
it, and the mechanism is wrong.** `grep -c try_mine tools/check_dig_hitch.gd` = **1**; there is no loop
around it (`sed -n '160,173p'` shows no `for`/`while`). It is a single call whose purpose is to *create one
dig event*, and the assertions beside it are `last_baked_cells <= MAX_DIG_CELLS` and
`pending_rows() == owed` — a **cell count and a row count**, not time. The layer's timed section wraps
`_bake_fine_region` in `Time.get_ticks_usec()`, best-of-N, and asserts a **per-cell ratio** of region bake
against full bake.

**That is a COST-PER-EVENT measurement, and the pacing defect cannot touch it.** Bypassing `_update_mining`
changes how OFTEN a dig happens; it does not change what one bake costs. So my original cut — *"compromised
if it reports something with TIME in the units"* — would have condemned a layer that is fine.

**THE CORRECTED CUT:** a fixture is compromised when it reports a **RATE, a DURATION-PER-PLAYER-ACTION, or
a SHARE OF A CYCLE** — anything whose value depends on how often the player can act. It is NOT compromised
when it reports the **cost of one event it triggered itself**, however that cost is timed. `check_frametime`
fails this because frame time under a dig *stream* depends on the stream rate; `check_dig_hitch` passes
because it times one bake.

**WHAT IS ACTUALLY STALE IN `check_dig_hitch`** is narrower and real: the docstring at `:20` still cites
*"DIG p95 33.8ms → 19.8ms"* — a frame-time p95, i.e. a figure of exactly the withdrawn class, sitting in a
layer whose own measurements are sound. **Not my file and not my lane;** reported to `c1`, who holds it.

**The lesson is symmetrical to the one that produced the class.** I named a rule, a peer applied it
faithfully, and the rule over-fired. Had I accepted the report — it flattered my own finding by making it
twice as large — PRIORITY would now record a false claim inside the correction whose entire purpose is
removing false claims. **The second time today that checking cost one grep.**

## A21. The guard that could not be false — and it was not only in the tests

**THE MEASUREMENT FIRST, because I have been wrong about "obviously" all week.** In Godot 4.6.2:

## A31 — `PC-01`: I built the failure I have been cataloguing all day, and the picture caught it

### The code already agreed with the ticket, in writing

Glyph, badge, progress bar, status lamp, need bubble, I/O ports. Correct for exactly as long as every

## A36 — three mechanisms for one bug, all three wrong

`check_grapple_reads` went red on 2 of 4 sweeps and green on every standalone run of the same commit. An
intermittent red is the worst shape a layer has, because it lands on whoever's change happens to be in the
tree when it fires.

**c1 proposed a mechanism.** Two clocks: the camera integrates in `_process(delta)`, every settle in the
fixture counts `physics_frame`, so contention removes frames from the clock the camera is actually on.
Elegant, and it came with a falsifier — print `Engine.get_frames_drawn()` either side of the settle.

**I ran it and it refuted them.** Idle, 30 physics awaits span **60** drawn frames (render ~120Hz, physics
60 — the ratio is inverted from the argument's assumption). In the failing sweep the first settle spanned
**32**. So the clock ratio genuinely moves. But 32 lerps at 12.5% each is 98.7% converged, and to leave
415 px on the table the jump would have to be ~31,000 px in a 4,096 px world. **The mechanism moves in the
right direction and is two orders of magnitude short of the number.** `main.gd:731` also snaps rather than
lerps any jump over half a viewport, which is every jump large enough to matter.

**Then I proposed one.** Twelve Godot windows, one frontmost, so `warp_mouse` into an unfocused window
must be failing and the read-back is the desktop cursor. Testable, and I said so.

**It refuted me too.** Two concurrent copies: the unfocused one warps to within **1.0 px** and passes.
macOS moves an unfocused window's pointer perfectly well.

**Twelve concurrent copies reproduced it — five red — on an assertion neither of us had been looking at:**

|  | signal | clock baseline |
|---|---|---|
| twelve concurrent | 4410–4604 px | **2845–4765 px** |
| three alone | 4622–4748 px | **1–4 px** |

**The signal is stable and the control is not.** The rope difference is ~4400–4750 px either way; the noise
floor it has to clear goes from three pixels to four thousand. That floor is two untouched captures
differenced, and the world's animation advances on `delta` — on wall time — while the fixture counts frames
between them. Contended, more animation phase elapses per frame, and the layer's estimate of "how much
moves when nothing is asked to move" grows until it swallows a signal that never moved.

This is not the familiar *a control that fails for the same reason as its subject*. **It is worse: the
control fails HARDER than its subject and takes the assertion down with it.** A noise floor that grows with
contention while the signal does not is an instrument that gets less sensitive exactly when the box is
busiest — which is when a sweep runs.

`add_excl`, and the comment says in as many words that it removes the **condition** and not the **cause**:
the 415 px cursor red remains unexplained, it did not reproduce in twelve concurrent runs, and the layer
will now be green with nobody having found out why it was not.

**Three mechanisms, two sessions, all three wrong, and the measurement found a fourth thing nobody had
proposed. Every one of the three had a story ready before its run.** The only thing that worked was making
the reproduction cheap enough to run twelve of them.

nearly nothing, so the ceremony clears a 24/255 threshold almost nowhere. A magnitude threshold is blind to

### The statistic I withdrew

> A statistic that swings eightfold between identical runs is not a strict measurement of a noisy thing.
> **It is a measurement of the threshold.**

And the heavy tail had a cause worth having: the tracer's window was five image px either side of the

## A46 — twelve rejection branches, zero controls: `c1`'s screen applied to my own guard

`c1` proposed a mechanical way to build a control that the author's blind spot cannot shape: **derive the
control's cases from the detector's own rejection branches.** Every `return ""` is a case the control is
obliged to contain. It needs no imagination, which is the point — the axis you cannot see is the axis you
will not invent a control for.

Applied to `tools/check_posed_fields.gd::_assigned_pair`. Its rejections:

    :233  empty line                          :233  line contains a `"`
    :236  no `=`                              :238  `==` `!=` `<=` `>=` `+=` `-=` `*=` `/=` `:=`
    :242  **bare assignment (no receiver)**   :242  `(` in the LHS
    :242  `[` in the LHS                      :242  space in the LHS
    :245  field is empty                      :245  field is not lower-case (a Constant or Type)
    :249  receiver is empty                   :249  receiver is not lower-case (an Autoload or Class)

Twelve categories of input silently dropped. The control corpus, in full (`:100-114`), is four assignments
plus a local and a `_ready` write. **It exercises `_scan_per_frame`'s scoping — `inside`, `locals` — and
the positive path. It contains not one line that lands in any of the twelve.**

> **Zero of twelve.** Nothing in this layer proves that any of those drops is intentional rather than
> accidental, and one of them — `dot < 1` — is the hole that let `check_snap_frame` pose `_cam_pos` past a
> guard built to catch exactly that.

**A second hole, reported with its population rather than without it.** The `"`-rejection at `:233` drops
the *entire line*, so an assignment whose RHS is a string literal is invisible — not just a comment
containing one, which is the documented purpose. Mechanism confirmed. **Reachability: empty today.** The

your own head, because the axis you cannot see is the axis you will not write into the fixture.** A control

## A50 — the cap reaches the player, and four paths that were not the one I was told about

`14df970 · 3b16de9 · d0e0155 · a7d5076 · 76c212c · b579c38 · b6a5a4f · d1a1777 · db6363b · 93bd934`
plus `ae695f3` / `ee35d8e` (capture + manifest) and `f0d83f7` (`history/151`, `152`).

**T1.0 is wired.** `PACK_BULK_CAP` and its four helpers had existed and been unit-tested for weeks with
nothing calling them, and the reason was structural rather than an oversight: twelve sites wrote
`inventory[x] = int(inventory.get(x, 0)) + n` inline, so there was no seam to enforce a rule at. **The rule
was not missing; the place to put it was.** `take_into_pack(item, n, spill_at)` is that place.

**The rule, and it needed a principle rather than a list.** A path that DESTROYS or REMOVES the thing
holding the material has nowhere to put it, so it spills down the column on `drop_item`'s own landing —
`mine`, the felling cascade, salvage. A path that leaves the thing standing REFUSES — the lode face keeps
its ore, the planted sapling keeps growing — because refusing there destroys nothing, so nothing needs a
floor to land on. `craft_item` is exempt and that is now written down as a decision: crafting spends bulk
from the same pack it deposits into and nets downward, so capping it could refuse a craft that would have
made room.

**`collect_ground` had to be capped or the whole mechanism was decorative.** A full pack spills onto the
floor and scoops the same units back on the next step; the return trip never happens and the cap reads as a
stutter in the pickup animation.

### Four findings, in descending order of how much they cost me

**1. I capped rock and missed ore, and c1 found it by reading the seam against its call sites.** `take_lode`
is the hand verb for an exposed ore face and it wrote the pack inline. A pack at exactly 90 went to **91 on
one click** and kept going. My layer asserted through `mine()` and `collect_ground()` and would have stayed
green however wrong that path was — an instrument that cannot register its subject, in my own new test.

**2. My first salvage repair destroyed forty units of ore.** Spilling while the machine was still in the
grid sent the overflow down its own column, `_column_landing` found THAT MACHINE, fed the units into the
buffer being emptied, and `clear()` deleted them. **The pack stayed under the cap the whole time.** Any
assertion phrased as "the pack did not grow" passes that. It was caught only because the layer asks where
the material WENT — which I had written into its header as a principle two hours earlier without expecting
to need it that fast.

**3. A test that named a function and never called it.** My felling fixture mined a lone wood block, passed,
and never reached `_fell_foliage_cell` — the cascade fires only when standing foliage loses its root. Four
blocks of trunk took a full pack to 93. The fixture has to build a tree.

**4. The order c1 insisted on was load-bearing.** Assertion first, watched going red against the unfixed
path, then the fix. Adding the fix first would have produced a green layer that proved nothing about a path
it had never asserted on. I would not have caught (2) either, because the salvage bug only shows up as a
FAIL on an assertion that was already written.

### Elsewhere

**The waterline said 46 and drew 105.** `WATER_RIPPLE_LEN = 46.0` sampled at 32px cell boundaries is below
Nyquist and folds to 105px. Fixed by a midpoint sample — 16px spacing, 46.0 renders as 46.0 — threaded
through the fill, the meniscus and the bright line together, since all three hang off the same points.
**No value below 64px could have been tuned into correctness**, because every one lands elsewhere on the

**UI-01 and UI-02 were both already shipped** and I planned both from the ledger before reading the source.
Fifth row in that table to survive the change that closed it. The table now says so about itself.

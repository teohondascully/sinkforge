## The 6a result — why the value approach is retired

**MEASURED.** `check_rock_reads` asks the blind tester's question as a number: sample cells the sim knows
are solid against cells it knows are air, both outside every light source, and report how often you would
be right guessing from pixels. A coin flip is 50%. The floor is 75%.

**MEASURED.** With a stable fixture, three runs per arm:

| arm | separability |
|---|---|
| `VOID_FLOOR` off | 58, 59, 59 |
| `VOID_FLOOR` on | 65, 65 |

**MEASURED.** Once carved space is excluded from the floor — which `check_room_reads` *requires*, because a
room must read brighter than the mass it was cut from — separability falls to 60% and the inversion
returns: air median 9.9 against rock 8.6.

**The conclusion, and it is the load-bearing one:** air cannot be one value, because air is legitimately
two things. A room you opened should announce itself; a natural void should read as absence. Two harness
layers assert opposite things about the same cells, and **no constant resolves a contradiction.** 6a does
not close on a brighter or darker field. Form and edge at the solid-to-air boundary is the only remaining
candidate.

**ASSUMED (load-bearing, unverified):** that a boundary signature *can* clear 75% where a field cue cannot.
I have not measured this. It is plausible because the tester's task is local — at the lamp's edge, is that
wall or hole — and a boundary cue is what a local task reads. It is not established, and if the local
measure also stalls near 60% then the problem is not the cue type and I will have retired an approach for
a reason that did not hold.

**PRE-REGISTERED, before the local measure exists:** 6a closes on the **global** measure. If a local
adjacent-pair measure comes up green while the global stays red, that is a finding about boundaries versus
fields — not a close. Written down so later-me has to delete a paragraph to cheat.

**THE NUMBERS ABOVE ARE PRE-LODE, and they are now historical.** c2's T0.2 work seeds **378 lode cells**
into every generated world, and `WorldRenderer` already stains a buried lode *through* the host rock
without asking whether the cell is solid. So the rock population my sampler draws from becomes **bimodal** —
plain rock and stained rock — sampled as if it were one thing. Kept rather than deleted: they describe a
world that is about to stop existing, and they are not wrong about that world. **The conclusion survives,
and not because I re-measured it**: it never rested on those numbers. It rests on a contradiction between
two layers' requirements, and a contradiction does not care what the field values are. Had the argument
been "60% is too low", c2's change would have voided it.

## 6b — the contact-edge instrument, pre-registered before it exists

**NOT WRITTEN YET.** Everything here is committed to before any number exists, because the whole failure
mode of 6a was choosing what counted as success after seeing what the world would give me.

**The question 6b asks, and why it is not 6a measured locally.** 6a asked *"given a patch of unlit pixels,
is that rock or air"* — a field question, answered by value. 6b asks whether the **boundary itself is
visible**, which is a question no field cue can answer at all. Split into two measures because they fail
independently:

| | measure | the tester's question |
|---|---|---|
| **6b-i** | edge **detectability** — gradient magnitude at true solid↔air boundaries vs at solid↔solid interiors, folded AUC | *is there an edge here at all?* |
| **6b-ii** | edge **polarity** — from the local asymmetry across a boundary, which side is rock, folded AUC | *which side of it is wall?* |

decide from a statistic** — that is what the naive vision agent is for, judging from pixels with no context.
The statistic is evidence for the arbiter, not the arbiter. Writing this down now because the temptation
after a green 6b will be to call T3.1 closed, and the pre-registration for 6a already says a local green
over a global red is a finding about boundaries versus fields.

**PRE-REGISTERED — the lode decision, made before seeing which way it moves a number.** Stained rock cells
are still rock, so excluding them would be measuring a world the player does not see. **6b samples them

that is a finding about ore tell doing legibility work, and it is reportable in its own right rather than
something to be averaged away.

**PRE-REGISTERED — which world.** 6b runs on a **generated** world and every assertion says so, per the
standing rule. It will be built after c2's lode push rather than before, because an instrument calibrated

re-baselined — which is how a moved number gets attributed to whatever was being tested that day.

**ASSUMED, load-bearing, and the one an auditor should attack:** that a boundary signature *can* clear 75%
where a field cue could not. Unmeasured. Plausible because the tester's task is local — at the lamp's edge,

### THE FIRST DEEP TREATMENT FAILED ITS INTENDED ASSERTION — reverted, not shipped

**VERIFIED, in `fine_terrain.gd`, and it explains the baseline exactly.** `_sky_form` — the term the rim

distinguishes neither. **The deep renderer's entire directional vocabulary is vertical.** A sky-facing face

### A DEFECT IN MY OWN VERDICT, found by the same run

**Pooled polarity is an invalid statistic for this subject and I did not notice when I designed it.**
`_draw_edge_ao` is a **key light**, not uniform occlusion — its own comment says "occlusion alone can't
make a form". It therefore makes rock **brighter** at a sky-facing face and **darker** at a ceiling, on
purpose. My polarity measure asks "is the rock side always the same side", which is a rule this renderer
deliberately breaks. **A perfectly executed key light scores ~50% on pooled polarity by construction.**

Same error as every population mistake in this project, one level up: the assumption was in the *verdict*
rather than in the sample.

**PRE-REGISTERED NOW, before any renderer change, because this change makes the bar EASIER and that has to
be declared in advance rather than discovered later:** when the verdict statistic moves, polarity becomes
**per-orientation**, at the same 75% floor, and **all three orientations must clear it** — not the best of
three, not the pooled number. Each carries its own 40-sample floor. Detectability is unaffected: it uses
|step| and is immune to orientation cancelling.

**Also fixed in passing:** `off` was `cell_px * FACE_OFFSET`, i.e. `CELL * zoom * FACE_OFFSET`, applied in
world space before the `* zoom` — so the true offset was `CELL * FACE_OFFSET * zoom²`. Correct at the zoom
1.0 every reading has been taken at, silently wrong at any other. Now in world units.

**FIXTURE DEFECT FOUND AND FIXED IN THE SAME RUN, recorded because it is the day's recurring shape.** The
first version inherited check_rock_reads' 16-row delve and 11×6 chamber and returned 29 rock|air faces
against the floor of 40. The reason was worse than the number: `floor_row` excludes everything at or above
`SURFACE_LINE + SURFACE_CLEAR` = row 42, and a 16-row delve from a surface at row 20 carves its chamber at
row ~36 — so **every cell of the carved space the layer exists to judge was discarded by the layer's own
depth filter before being measured.** The 29 surviving faces were incidental geometry elsewhere in frame.
Fixed by delving to 30 rows and widening the chamber to 41 (the lamp's widest veil cut is 9 cells, so an
11-wide room centred on the body is lit end to end); `MIN_SAMPLES`, `SURFACE_CLEAR`, `DARK_CEILING` and
`READ_FLOOR` all unchanged. **This is the third fixture today that was not standing where its effect is** —
after check_fastforward's runway in a rift and its machine on the walk line.

## THE CAPTURE LENS WAS WRONG, AND IT WAS WRONG TOWARD MY OWN CONCLUSION

**MEASURED.** `project.godot` sets `viewport 1280x720`, `window_width_override 1920x1080`,
`stretch/mode="canvas_items"`. The canvas is drawn at 1280x720 and composited **1.5x** into the 1920x1080
framebuffer that `get_texture().get_image()` returns. Four capture layers projected world to pixel by hand as
`(world - cam) * zoom + image_size * 0.5`, which assumes the captured image and the canvas are one space.

    image 1920x1080, viewport (1280,720), canvas scale (1,1), _current_zoom 1.000
    body cell        hand-rolled (960, 516)   engine (640, 336) -> true image (960, 504)
    20 cells right   hand-rolled (1600, 516)  engine (1280, 336) -> true image (1920, 504)

## 6a: THE CUE IS THE WALL, NOT THE ROCK -- A NEGATIVE RESULT WITH A KNOCKOUT

6a's failure is the INTERIOR (n=302 at 56%, chance); the BOUNDARY arm already passes (n=79 at 79%). So the
question is what carries the rock/air distinction in unlit rock, and whether any constant can carry it to 75%.

**A PREDICTION WAS REGISTERED IN SOURCE BEFORE THE SWEEP, AND THE SWEEP REFUTED IT.** I predicted that
raising rock's grain would move rock's texture and barely move separability. It moved it, downward, and
monotonically:

    SF_ROCK_GRAIN   1.0 -> 62%    3.0 -> 56%    6.0 -> 51%

The reason is the one I had not considered: **the two populations were separated by rock being SMOOTHER than
air** (rock 1.79, air 2.05), so amplifying rock's grain drives it up through air's value and through the
crossover, where separability is 50% by construction. Predicting the magnitude and missing the SIGN is still
a refutation.

**ROCK'S GRAIN IS NEARLY ABSENT FROM UNLIT ROCK.** Zeroing GRAIN_AMP outright:

    SF_ROCK_GRAIN   0.0 -> rock grain 1.74 (from 1.79), separability 63% (from 62%)

Two octaves of noise per fine cell, and about 3% of what a dark rock face actually shows. **Scoped strictly:
this is measured OUTSIDE THE LAMP, which is this layer's whole population. It says nothing about lit rock**,
where the term may be doing exactly its job. Stating the boundary because the failure to state one is what
this project keeps catching.

**THE CUE IS THE WALL, AND A KNOCKOUT PROVES IT:**

    SF_WALL_GRAIN   air grain   GRAIN sep   plain BOUNDARY arm
         0.0          1.72         50%           78%      <- knockout: pure chance
         1.0          2.05         62%           79%      <- shipped
         2.5          2.82         70%           76%
         5.0          4.07         73%           70%      <- below its own floor

At zero the layer cannot tell rock from air at all. Rock's grain holds 1.79-1.80 across every setting. **The
back wall's texture is the entire mechanism**, and rock contributes nothing to it.

**AND IT IS A TRADE, NOT A LEVER.** Amplifying the wall darkens air's median value (9.4 -> 7.6 -> 7.4) toward
rock's 8.4, so the value-based partition collapses (interior arm 58% -> 53% -> 51%) and the BOUNDARY arm --
the one that currently PASSES -- falls from 79% to 70%, under the unchanged floor. Eleven points of grain
separability bought for nine points off the arm that already works, and still short of 75%.

**CONCLUSION: "no constant closes 6a" is re-evidenced from a direction nobody had tried** -- two constants,
swept both ways across 0.0 to 6.0, with a knockout control, and the better cue never leaves the 50-73% band

restatement. c2 had restored that sentence after I talked them out of it on a single-viewpoint figure, and
the sweep says they were right twice over.

distinction is currently ONE-SIDED: it lives entirely on the air side, in a wall whose texture also carries

## T5.6, partial — the suite's ratio assertions, audited by DIRECTION

T5.2's shape generalises: a ratio computed over a population nobody checked. So I swept every
`tools/check_*.gd` for ratios that are *asserted on*, and classified them. **18 across the suite.** The
useful discovery is not a defect list, it is a rule:

**WHETHER A COLLAPSED POPULATION FAILS SAFE OR FAILS OPEN IS DECIDED BY THE ASSERTION'S DIRECTION.** A
denominator guard -- `maxf(steps, 1.0)`, `maxi(rows_total, 1)` -- does not protect the claim, it only
prevents a divide-by-zero and hands back a number. That number is **zero**. So:

- `ratio >= floor` (a lower bound) **fails safe.** The population collapses, the ratio goes to 0, the floor
  is missed, the layer goes red. `check_relief`'s `walkable`/`mean_step`, `check_richness`'s `density`,
  `check_plunge`, `check_traverse` -- all of these.
- `ratio <= cap` (an upper bound) **fails OPEN.** Same collapse, same 0, and 0 is comfortably under any cap.
  The layer goes green *because* its subject stopped existing.

Seven upper-bound ratios exist. Four are not populations at all (`check_pack_layout`'s `world_aspect` is a
geometric identity; `check_progressive_bake`'s denominator is a compile-time grid size with `opening > 0`
asserted; `check_seam` is two-sided so a collapse fails its MIN). Two have a completion veto already --
`check_agility` asserts `reached`, `check_dig_hitch` asserts `cells > 0`.

**THE ONE THAT DID NOT WAS MINE, WRITTEN AN HOUR EARLIER.** `check_snap_frame` divides both arms by a
`structured` population it never checked. Forcing that population to collapse (STRUCT_TOL 6 -> 200):

    SAME bucket: 0 of 206 structured pixels changed (0.00%)
    NEXT bucket: 194 of 208 structured pixels changed (93.27%)

**Both assertions pass, and they pass BETTER than on the real run** -- a perfect 0.00% noise floor against a
93% control, the best numbers the layer has ever printed, computed over 206 pixels out of 155,000. That is

## The grade was eating the terrain, one bake at a time

`0de1bbb` (fix), `901cf34` (guard). A band of pure red and pure green ran the full width of the frame along
the walked surface line. It was on main, it predated the tooth, and it was the most obviously wrong thing
in the game's own promotional capture.

The coarse terrain bake renders into a SubViewport that **retains** its render target between updates --
`CLEAR_MODE_NEVER` on the partial-bake path, which is what makes a dig cost one chunk instead of the whole
world. That viewport also inherited the `WorldEnvironment`, whose adjustment pass runs as a viewport
post-process. So saturation 1.18 was re-applied to the same stored pixels on every bake update, and the
terrain compounded **1.18^n**:

    boot (87,130,47) -> (87,143,35) -> (87,160,19) -> (87,182,0) -> (80,255,0) -> ... -> (42,255,0)

Green climbs, red and blue fall. The fine layer covers the coarse bake everywhere except the walked line,
so it did not read as a discoloured world -- it read as a neon stripe exactly where the ground meets the
sky. One line: the bake viewport gets its own world. Primaries 23945 -> 1055, surface band 22842 -> 208,
bake colour-change events per arc 24 -> 0, with the grade still fully on.

### Four theories died first, each killed by its own test

1. The WorldEnvironment and the lens. **The 2x2 left 21131 primaries with BOTH off.** I had been calling
   them one thing; the lens is not even on the Environment.
2. Premultiplied accumulation across partial bakes -- eight repaints, zero drift.
3. The partial bake leaving clean chunks dirty -- it hides them and erases the rects first. Correct code.
4. In-place mutation of shared `MaterialDef` colours -- no such write exists in the tree.

What worked was giving up on proposing another HOW and instrumenting WHEN: a watcher that sampled the bake
every twenty frames during the arc and printed only on change. The monotonic walk named the operator in one
run. **Four mechanisms proposed from reading code, zero correct; one measurement of timing, decisive.**

### And I withdrew my own founding measurement

My earlier note said the saturation grade caused a pure-primary strip, with controls reading 0, 6 and 23

## Why eighty layers passed a frame with a stripe across it

This is the part worth keeping. Not one layer was pointed the wrong way. **Every layer in the suite
photographs a freshly booted world** -- boot, settle, shutter -- and the defect was a per-bake operator, so

That is the same measurement-boundary error as everything else I found today, at suite scale: the

## T2.3's gate: the real input path IS drivable, and the rate is roughly one block a second

**Status: partial. No number is claimed, and the fixture is not rewritten.** Recording what is established
so the next pass does not redo it.

`check_frametime.gd:490` calls `_main.try_mine(target)` once per frame and `_update_mining` is never

this is not one fixture's problem: every fixture in the project mines through the spatial verb and none of
them pays the temporal charge.

**The real path is drivable, and I have it working.** `Controls.pressed` is `Input.is_action_pressed` behind
a `deaf` flag, so a fixture can set `Controls.deaf = false`, hold `Input.action_press(Controls.MINE)`, and

    i= 0  pressed=true  _aim=(51,19)  workable=true  can_reach=true  charge=0.017
    i= 8  ...                                                        charge=0.150
    i=16  ...                                                        charge=0.283
    i=24  ...                                                        charge=0.417

**Charge accumulates ~0.0167 per frame, so a block takes ~60 frames — about one second** for this material
at the wood pickaxe, against a direct-call fixture that can break one per frame. That is the order of the
discrepancy the ticket predicted, and it is now measured rather than assumed. It is NOT yet the replacement
baseline: one material, one tool, no rhythm or recovery state established, and the ticket is explicit that a
figure quoted without those is the thing that was withdrawn.

**Two real obstacles found on the way, both invisible to any `try_mine` fixture:**

1. **With a placeable selected, LMB builds — it does not mine.** `_effective_aim` returns the raw cell when

   the shape that makes the rate dig-limited.

**Where it is stuck, and it is my probe rather than the game:** the two-arm version walks the body to a
fresh column first, and in that variant the body ends up too far from the cell it targets --

walk first, works. So the staging is wrong, not the driver.

**The probe refuses to report a rate when nothing was dug**, and that is the only reason this is an honest

## The rope's pay-out, measured for the first time — and no layer shipped from it

The ticket I claimed after the peer trimmed *"S pays out to swing wide"*: `PAY_SPEED = 520.0` and the
symmetry law at `grapple.gd:392` (*"paying out scales tangential speed DOWN by the same law, which makes S
a real brake and swinging wide a real decision rather than a free way to cover ground"*) are a designed
decision with a stated cost and **no runner**. First numbers, on the pump rig, holding one axis throughout:

    arm         len px   reach px   LATE reach   tangential px/s   half-swings
    hold           388        226           63               362             9
    PAY OUT        480        227           68               328             7
    reel in         26        227           14               191             3

**Two instrument lessons before any of it means anything.**

First version reported reach as a max over the whole window and got 226 / 227 / 227 — identical to a pixel
across three arms whose rope lengths differ by a quarter. That is not a null result, it is the wrong
statistic: **a pendulum's widest excursion is its FIRST**, set by where you let go, and damping makes every
later one smaller. A max over the window reports the starting condition and is structurally blind to
anything the rope does afterwards. Same family as everything else this week — the population sampled was
not the population the claim is about.

Late-window reach does move: **+8% reach for −9% tangential speed.** Directionally the design is real —
paying out is a trade, not a free ride — but 8% is not a magnitude a player feels, and I am not going to
write that sentence as a finding yet, **because the arms are not comparable**: they ran 9, 7 and 3
half-swings before hitting the cycle cap, so the late windows cover different amounts of swing. Comparing a
late window across arms of different duration is a confound I introduced and have not removed.

**So no layer ships from this.** A gate whose floors came from a confounded measurement is the thing I have
spent two days finding in other people's work, and a threshold set to agree with a number I already
distrust is worse than no threshold. What the next pass needs is arms matched on swing COUNT rather than
frame budget, and a reach statistic taken at a fixed swing index rather than over a window.

Recorded rather than dropped because the headline stands on its own and did not need the layer: **the
symmetric half of the winch has never been measured, and the first look says the decision it is supposed to
offer is worth about eight percent.**

## Two 6a mechanisms proposed, two refuted, both by me, before spending any machine time

The machine was held all session, so I took 6a apart on pixels I already had. `_moment_delve.png` and
`_moment_delve_zoom.png`, decoded in pure python — this box has neither numpy nor PIL — restricted to the
dark region the ticket is about, mean patch luma <= the layer's own `DARK_CEILING` of 34.

**First: GRAIN is ramp-dominated.** `_patch_stats` returns a raw standard deviation, and a std cannot tell
a TEXTURE from a smooth ILLUMINATION GRADIENT — both make pixels differ from the patch mean. The veil is a
stretched lightmap on `TEXTURE_FILTER_LINEAR`, the lamp falls off radially, post_fx multiplies a vignette:
gradients on rock and air alike. If that shared ramp dominated, every rock-only treatment would be divided
into a large common denominator, which explains the 58 -> 55 -> 59 -> 62 history exactly. Fitting a plane
per patch and removing it:

    patch r    std     ramp    resid   lap     ramp share of GRAIN variance
    10        2.01    0.98    1.71    1.31            24%
    21        2.77    1.63    2.07    1.32            34%
    30        3.33    2.06    2.43    1.33            38%

Real, grows with patch size as a gradient must, and NOT the story. A perfect removal takes 2.77 -> 2.07,
which does not carry 62% to 75%. **Refuted.**

**Second: the dark is pixel-scale speckle, so the four failures were the wrong SCALE not the wrong
amplitude.** I read that out of lap 1.32 against resid 2.07, and the tooth is `hash(floor(world_pos))`, the
finest octave available, with its coarse companion at `mottle_add = 0.006` — a quarter of the fine term.
A clean prediction with a cheap test, box-averaging before taking the std:

    scale    1px    2px    4px    8px   16px
    std     2.77   2.44   2.17   1.96   1.26
    vs 1px  100%    88%    78%    71%    45%

71% survives an 8x8 average. The dark is BROADBAND. **Refuted, and by a worse error than the first one** —
I turned "lap 1.32 is lower than pure white noise would give" into "almost all the energy is high
frequency", which is the opposite of what that comparison says. The Laplacian was consistent with a mix all
along and I read a mixture as an extreme.

What survives is a baseline, and I will take it: dark-region patch means, median luma **8.59**, spread
ACROSS patches **7.21**, within-patch **2.80**. Chroma b-r on the same patches: median **5.26**, across-patch
spread **5.94** — comparable to luma and independent of it, which is the interesting part.

**EVERY NUMBER ABOVE IS PRE-TOOTH.** `3c46c8c` regenerated those captures at 14:03 on 2026-08-17;
`7181e04` landed `rock_tooth.gdshader` at 23:44:57 the same night. I nearly wrote them up as current.

one. `check_machine_state`'s `D_motion` pair spans **22 frames**, inside which an unsettled lamp traverses

**A DERIVATIVE IS NOT A RESIDUAL, and this one is vacuous under a freeze.** The peer's settle watches
`now.distance_to(prev) < EPS`. At `time_scale = 0`, delta is 0, so `1 - exp(0) = 0` and the lerp is the
identity: `_lamp_offset` **cannot** move, `now == prev` exactly, `held` reaches its hold count, and the
loop reports SETTLED on a lamp frozen mid-ease. It is correct where it stands (that layer never freezes)
and it is a trap for the eleven layers it would be carried to, two of which freeze. The fix is to assert a
RESIDUAL — `_lamp_offset.distance_to(lamp_target())` — from an accessor the renderer's own `_process`
calls, so the fixture cannot re-derive the projection and drift from it.

**Freezing pickles the transient; it does not resolve it.** A freeze N frames after a disturbance leaves
the pool `e0*exp(-9N/60)` from where it belongs and holds it there for every capture in the window. Two
arms frozen at different N carry different lighting as a **systematic offset, not noise** — and no
same-vs-same control inside one frozen window can detect it, because within the window it is perfectly
constant. Freezing also removes the pointer's LARGEST effect (the 9-cell veil cut, the CELL*5.6 halo) and
leaves the small ones live (reticle, ghost, reach colouring, HUD hover), so a frozen differencing layer's
stillness control registers a stray hand as a few reticle pixels while being blind to the light source.

**Pinning the lamp for fixtures is the wrong fix, twice.** A fixture assigning `_lamp_offset` has posed
nothing — `_process` overwrites it unconditionally every frame, which is the exact pattern
`check_posed_fields.gd` exists to catch. And a pin would blind the only twelve layers that photograph the
frame to every lamp regression there is. Pose the pointer, settle the lamp, assert the residual.

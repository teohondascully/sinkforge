# VIBE_AUDIT_RESPONSE

Independent subjective audit, 2026-08-17, against HEAD `94ac6fb`. Read-only; no retained repository
changes. Recorded here verbatim in substance because it is the evidence behind `docs/PRIORITY.md` and
because its scores are the baseline any later claim of improvement has to beat.

**Method note from the auditor:** canonical moments, 124 visual-history PNGs and the hand-authored sprites
were inspected **before** any design document was read. A fresh disposable-copy capture was taken from
`94ac6fb`; the teach capture failed to catch its corner and photographed an ordinary "Forge 2 ingots"
objective instead — *that proves the capture fixture failed on this run, not that the tutorial fails in
human play.* Soundstage is graded from design and source only: no listening or mix test was performed.
`check_rock_reads` and the full harness were not run.

---

## The three findings the auditor would defend hardest

1. **SINKFORGE becomes less interesting at the exact moment it announces its thesis.** "THE LINE RUNS"
   should *begin* the compulsion loop. Instead the production graph has six recipes, three routing verbs,
   terminal gear and plate outputs and one bulk door — after which the player's next substantial activity
   is choosing a column and holding dig.
2. **The memorable game is not Factorio × Terraria; it is a kinetic industrial-descent game.** The image
   that survives to tomorrow is the tiny yellow miner hanging from a bent gold rope in `_moment_bend.png`
   — not any factory, machine, material or production line.
3. **The presentation system is currently the art director.** In nearly every canonical frame the
   objective plate, depth badge, zone title, inventory rail and gold typography are clearer, brighter and
   more authored than the underground world they are explaining.

> **The harsh sentence:** SINKFORGE has found a silhouette, but it has not found a soul; dangling over
> darkness feels like its game, while building a factory still feels like its tutorial.

## Per-dimension highest-leverage change

1. **Thumbnail 4.7** — one HUD-free hero frame: miner large on a visibly bending gold rope above a live,
   illuminated gravity-fed chain crossing a distinctive stratum landmark.
2. **Era 5.2** — replace the rectangular world-machine vocabulary with a small authored set of strongly
   silhouetted, materially animated machines. *The world reads 2015–2018; the Bazaar reads 2024–2026.*
3. **Personality 4.4** — make the Bazaar a recurring physical **character**: an unmistakable attendant or
   apparatus whose opinions and silhouette persist across progression.
4. **Addiction 3.3** — make the first automated line immediately feed a recurring **two-input consumer**
   whose visible starvation creates the next routing decision. *First put-down point: immediately after the
   celebration of the first automated ingot.*
5. **First 90s 5.0** — compress the opening into three beats — **mine, place, watch** — and get the first
   self-running material conversion on screen before 60 seconds. Currently ~110s for a competent newcomer.
6. **Verb Feel 5.4** — see table above.
7. **Material Honesty 5.6** — give every solid-to-air boundary a dependable value-and-edge signature
   outside lamp influence, **without adding more surface grain**.
8. **Sprite Craft 6.2** — redraw anticipation / contact / recovery keyframes around changing torso angle
   and centre of mass, keeping the existing palette and silhouette.
9. **Placeholder 4.1** — replace the visible world machines with one coherent authored sprite kit before
   spending another pass on terrain overlays.
10. **Diegetic 3.7** — remove the persistent top objective slab after its first lesson; attach subsequent
    guidance to the relevant world object. *~85–90% of the interface floats above the world.*
11. **Lore 2.2** — put one industrial remnant in each band whose history **visually teaches that band's
    new mechanical rule**. Deleting the fiction today would barely change play.
12. **World Coherence 5.6** — make geological structure causally predict resources and hazards: visible
    faults should generate both ore concentration and water intrusion.
13. **Descent 6.6** — give every band one exclusive full-frame landmark or physical behaviour that cannot
    appear in the band above it. *Surface-vs-deep is legible; 15m vs 27m is not.*
14. **Surprise 3.2** — one early two-input machine that emits a **waste stream**, forcing supply *and*
    disposal as a new recurring rule. *Novelty half-life is ~15–25 minutes.*
15. **Soundstage 7.8** — add source-to-listener **rock-mass occlusion** with progressive low-pass and
    attenuation. Also: the silence is almost continuously occupied, which may weaken contrast.
16. **Cruelty 4.2** — one reversible flood consequence that can interrupt a route or machine without
    deleting inventory. *The game is generous about loss but accidentally punitive about time.*
17. **Menu Craft 6.9** — keep the information hierarchy, but stage it on a visible physical Bazaar counter
    with attendant and world silhouette readable behind it. *No longer "2003 coded" — but it could sell
    accounting software with a palette change.* WORKS has the clearest hierarchy; PACK wastes space.
18. **Ambition 4.8** — **stop using Factorio × Terraria as the operative design test.** Require every major
    feature to strengthen "kinetic industrial descent."
19. **Name Recall 6.2** — make the dead cog-ring / Sinkforge mechanism a gigantic recurring structure the
    player crosses, repairs and eventually awakens across multiple bands.
20. **Fun Tax 3.5** — after the first automated line succeeds, **permanently retire its manual hauling
    task** instead of asking the player to repeat it at a larger number.

## Synthesis 1 — the kill list (delete or suspend, in this order)

1. The permanent giant objective plate. Keep for first exposure, then kill it.
2. The public **Factorio × Terraria** promise.
3. Terminal gear and plate production — collapse into construction costs unless they become recurring sinks.
4. The standalone full-screen **PACK** tab in its current form.
5. Recurring full-width band ceremonies over live movement.
6. The persistent bottom-left keyboard legend.
7. Any new lore campaign, bosses, or ending work.
8. **Any new harness layer not attached to a human-observed failure or a chosen visible change.**
9. Marketing screenshots centred on menus, empty shafts, or tiny machines.
10. The assumption that more terrain grain will solve solidity. *The frame already has grain; it needs form.*

## Synthesis 2 — if I had one week

| # | Task | Cost | Why it is placed here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Observe the first real stop point.** 3–5 unfamiliar players, fresh save, no coaching. Record time of first delight, first confusion, first automation, first voluntary detour, first "now what?" Do not ask whether they liked it until they stop. | ½ day | The project has extensive agent timing and **no human cohort** |
| 2 | **Replace the post-automation void.** First line feeds a visible two-input recurring consumer — ideally a **Descent Engine** needing plates and fuel continuously, visibly starving when either path fails, whose output changes physical descent. | 2 days | The first automation must create a problem, not complete a checklist |
| 3 | **Make automation retire labour.** Remove the need to repeat the ore-to-forge haul. Later labour must be qualitatively different. | ½ day | Lowers the fun tax; makes automation psychologically true |
| 4 | **Fix the honest DIG stall.** Work from the work-proven 32–35 ms baseline, not the withdrawn green numbers. | 1 day | Do not add more break particles until the action holds frame time |
| 5 | **Remove the visual supervisor.** Shrink/retire the objective slab, remove the key legend, stop zone text colliding with map and action. | ½ day | A **subtraction** pass, not a UI redesign |
| 6 | **Give the factory one hero machine.** One large animated machine: unmistakable silhouette, visible inputs, moving material, startup behaviour, sound identity. Use it for the consumer from #2. | 1 day | Prove one machine can own a frame; do not resprite the catalogue |
| 7 | **Re-test and recapture.** Same build, new players. Ask what the game is and what they wanted to do next. | 1 day | Capture a thumbnail only after someone independently describes the desired second goal |

> **The week succeeds if players finish the first line and immediately form their own plan — not if
> another score turns green.**

## Synthesis 3 — where the effort has actually gone

**Across the whole project the answer is NOT "almost entirely invisible infrastructure."** The 124
visual-history frames, sprite overhaul, lighting, terrain, water, rope, Bazaar, map, galleries, lode
presentation and 1,213-line feel history are a sustained and unusually large visible-art campaign. The
artifact is materially better than its earliest programmer-grid frames.

**But the latest audit run has crossed the line:**

- From `1d18edf` to HEAD: **93 commits**.
- **55** commit subjects match infrastructure/audit/test/instrumentation work; **30** match visible or
  gameplay work (categories overlap).
- **77 of 108 changed paths are under `tools/` — 71.3%.**
- Line counts: `tools/` 21,790 · `scenes/` 15,892 · `src/` 5,588 · `tests/` 4,161 · `docs/` 8,401.

Some of that was justified — it found a harness capable of destroying a real player save, gauges that
rewarded broken mining, contaminated fixtures, and measurements that did not prove their subjects. Those
are real failures, not bureaucracy.

> **The ratio is nevertheless wrong now. SINKFORGE has instrumentation abundance and human-evidence
> scarcity.** It can measure whether a scripted agent encounters "news"; it does not know whether a human
> reads that news as a reason to keep playing. The next marginal week should spend almost nothing on new
> instrumentation. Use the existing harness only to protect changes chosen from human observation. The
> unmeasured risk is no longer whether the code does what it says — it is **whether the player wants what
> the code does.**

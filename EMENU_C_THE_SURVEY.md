# SINKFORGE — E-menu proposal C: THE SURVEY

Generate ONE image, 1920 x 1080, a game interface mockup. Not a collage, not a storyboard.

This is a visual design study for a game menu. The quantities below are a fixture for the drawing.

## The game in four lines

Sinkforge is a side-on pixel-art excavation and factory game. A field engineer digs beneath a permanent
surface rig. Gravity carries goods down the passages the player cuts: the hole is a conveyor. Machines
process and lift material, and the rig issues orders for processed goods.

## The idea this image is testing

A diagram is omniscient. A record is partial. This menu is the engineer's own survey sheet, and it
shows **only what they have walked**. Where the survey stops, the paper is blank.

That single property is what separates a notebook from an infographic, and it is the point of this
image.

## Layout

One working sheet filling most of the screen, with the dimmed pixel-art shaft visible around its edges.

**The right two-thirds is the survey.** The excavation drawn as a hand-measured section: the Drill, the
ore chute with its real bends, the collection cavity, the Processor, the Coal Hopper and its separate
chute, the Winch Head, the powered return, the surface rig. Depths written by hand down a ruled margin.

**And the survey stops.** Below minus 16 metres on the left third of the sheet, the drawn rock ends in
a ragged surveyed edge and becomes blank paper. A pencilled query mark sits in the blank. The player
has not been down there, so it is not drawn.

**The left third is the tally**, kept in two hands. Freight in ink, five entries, with a running total
written at the foot and underlined twice: **48 / 90**. Kit in pencil below a ruled line, three entries,
lighter, uncounted, with the word "free" beside the rule.

**One entry has been annotated in a hurried hand:** coal is circled, and a drawn arrow runs from it
across to the Processor, with "out of fuel" written beside the machine and a small red triangle
inked above both. The live state of the world, rendered as something the engineer scribbled.

**The Glimmer has a small careful study in the margin**, better drawn than anything else on the page,
because it is the one thing the engineer drew for pleasure. Beneath it: "No workshop use recorded.
Kept it anyway."

**Lower left, the selected item:** a small exploded sketch of the Plate Press, "2 Iron ingots to 1
Plate", and a hand-ruled box around the words **Place in shaft**, inked heavier than the rest.

**The order, upper left**, six small squares in a row drawn by hand: two filled solid, three hatched,
one left empty, under "Ingots for the winch".

## The fixture

FREIGHT, 48 of 90 carried: Clay 24 · Coal 12 · Iron-bearing ore 8 · Iron ingot 3 · Glimmer 1
KIT, free: Torch 4 · Rope 2 · Plate Press 1

## Palette and rendering

The paper is a cool grey-buff working stock, #CFC8BA, not cream and not parchment. It has tooth and
faint grid ruling, and it has been folded once.

Ink is the game's own: graphite #3A3D45 for the drawing, brass #CCA84D for measurements and totals,
steel #6B7A9E for the ore, a single red #F54233 for the annotation. The glimmer study is the only
saturated colour on the sheet, #3DC7D2.

Type is a drafting hand: engineering capitals for labels, a fast cursive for the annotations, and
tabular figures for counts. Instruments used unevenly: a straightedge that slipped once, a struck-out
correction, a measurement written twice.

Keep the rendering restrained. Small precise marks, a lot of quiet paper, one warm lamp-light falling
across the sheet from the upper left.

Show the sheet at rest, settled and sharp.

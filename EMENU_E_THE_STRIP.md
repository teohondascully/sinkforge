# SINKFORGE — E-menu proposal E: THE STRIP

Generate ONE image, 1920 x 1080, a game interface mockup. Not a collage, not a storyboard.

This is a visual design study for a game menu. The quantities below are a fixture for the drawing.

## The game in four lines

Sinkforge is a side-on pixel-art excavation and factory game. A field engineer digs beneath a permanent
surface rig. Gravity carries goods down the passages the player cuts: the hole is a conveyor. Machines
process and lift material, and the rig issues orders for processed goods.

## The idea this image is testing

Perhaps the full-screen menu should not exist. Pressing E grows the hotbar the player already has,
in place, along the bottom of an undimmed world. Nothing covers the shaft. The player never leaves.

This is the argument that the fastest menu is the one that is barely a menu, and it exists so we can
see how much the full sheet was actually buying.

## Layout

**The world fills the entire frame, undimmed and fully rendered:** a side-on pixel-art cross-section of
the shaft, the engineer standing at minus 20 metres beside the ore chute, machines at work above and
below them, warm lamp light, dug space near black. This is a game screenshot with interface on it.

**Along the bottom, where the hotbar already lives, it has grown upward into two rows.**

Top row, FREIGHT: five square wells, keybind digits 1 to 5, each with its drawn silhouette and count.
Running beneath the row, a thin brass load bar filled to 48 of 90 with the figure at its right end.

Bottom row, KIT: three square wells, digits 6 to 8, sitting below a hairline with no bar beneath them
and a small "no load" beside them.

**Above the selected well**, a single compact card floats: Plate Press, "2 Iron ingots to 1 Plate", and
one filled brass button reading **Place in shaft**.

**Down the right edge, a narrow vertical spine, about 9 percent of the width.** This is the entire
factory, compressed: a depth ruler from 0 to minus 30 metres with small machine ticks at minus 12,
minus 18, minus 24 and minus 28, each tick wearing its machine's lamp colour, and a brass caret at
minus 20 showing where the engineer is. Above the ruler, the order as six pips, two filled solid,
three ringed hollow, one empty.

**The join happens in the world, not in the interface.** The coal well wears a small red upward
triangle, and far below in the same frame the Processor's own lamp burns the same red, visible through
the rock. The player makes the connection by looking at the game.

## The fixture

FREIGHT, 48 of 90 carried: Clay 24 · Coal 12 · Iron-bearing ore 8 · Iron ingot 3 · Glimmer 1
KIT, free: Torch 4 · Rope 2 · Plate Press 1

Well 5 in the freight row is not the selection. The Plate Press sits in the kit row and it is the lit
well: brass border, faint brass wash.

## The world in this frame

The surface rig is off the top of the frame. The visible span runs from about minus 6 metres to minus
30. The Drill is upper left, the ore chute descends past the engineer, the Coal Hopper is right of it,
the Processor sits at minus 24 burning red, and the Winch Head at minus 28 is at the bottom edge with
its powered return climbing out of frame on the right.

## Palette and rendering

Rock reads warm clay brown #AD7A4F near the top of the frame, cooling to grey-blue at depth. Dug space
near black. Torchlight pools warm.

Interface ground #12141D, wells #1C1F29, edges #333B4D with a #8594AD top bevel. Brass accent #CCA84D.
Text #CCD4E3, dim #8A94A8.

Item inks: clay #AD7A4F · coal #3D404A · iron-bearing ore #6B7A9E · iron ingot #CCD6EB · glimmer
#3DC7D2 · rope #C7A870 · torch #FFC25C.

Machine lamps: starved of fuel #F54233, starved of input #F7B838, working #59EB6B, idle #858C9E.

The game authors its interface on a 640 x 360 grid and presents it at 2x. Keep every interface element
small, crisp and on-grid. The world is the large thing in this image.

Show the screen at rest, settled and sharp.

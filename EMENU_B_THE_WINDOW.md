# SINKFORGE — E-menu proposal B: THE WINDOW

Generate ONE image, 1920 x 1080, a game interface mockup. Not a collage, not a storyboard.

This is a visual design study for a game menu. The quantities below are a fixture for the drawing.

## The game in four lines

Sinkforge is a side-on pixel-art excavation and factory game. A field engineer digs beneath a permanent
surface rig. Gravity carries goods down the passages the player cuts: the hole is a conveyor. Machines
process and lift material, and the rig issues orders for processed goods.

## The idea this image is testing

The world keeps running while this menu is open. So the menu is not a document about the factory, it is
a **window into** it, and the inventory is a rail at the edge of that window.

The factory is rendered the way the world is rendered: dark, underground, lit by the machines' own
lamps. It is not a diagram on paper. Material is visibly in motion.

## Layout

**The shaft cutaway fills roughly 78 percent of the screen**, running nearly edge to edge, seen as a
lit cross-section through the earth. The engineer is on it. Ore is caught mid-fall in the chute, the
winch drum is caught mid-turn, and the Coal Hopper is part drained so its level reads as a quantity.
Depths are marked along the left in small brass figures.

**A slim vertical rail down the left edge, about 18 percent wide.** The eight carried stacks as small
square wells stacked down it, each with its icon and count and its keybind digit. At the foot of the
rail, a thin brass load bar reading 48 / 90, with the five freight stacks contributing to it and the
three kit stacks sitting below the bar's reach, visibly outside the accounting.

**The gesture that carries the whole design:** the Processor at minus 24 metres burns red in the world,
and the coal well in the rail wears the same red upward triangle, and a single fine hairline connects
the two across the screen. One glyph in two places, joined.

**Bottom right, two small chips over the world**, not cards on a sheet: the order as six pips in a row
with two filled solid, three filled hollow and one empty outline, under the words "Ingots for the
winch"; and beside it a small glimmer crystal with "Kept it anyway."

**The selected item, Plate Press,** appears as a compact chip at the foot of the rail: the machine drawn
small, and one filled brass button reading **Place in shaft**.

## The fixture

FREIGHT, 48 of 90 carried: Clay 24 · Coal 12 · Iron-bearing ore 8 · Iron ingot 3 · Glimmer 1
KIT, free: Torch 4 · Rope 2 · Plate Press 1

Plate Press is the selected well, lit brass. The coal well wears the red triangle.

## The shaft, drawn the same way in every proposal

Surface rig at top right with a Winch Station beside it. Drill at minus 12 metres on the left. An
excavated ore chute descends from the Drill, bending twice, widening into a collection cavity above the
Processor at minus 24 metres. A Coal Hopper at minus 18 metres sits right of the ore chute and sends a
separate coal chute down to meet the Processor's intake at a marked junction. The Processor's output
descends right to a Winch Head at minus 28 metres. A powered return climbs the right-hand side from the
Winch Head to the Winch Station. A dotted route leaves the Winch Station and arrives at the Surface Rig,
labelled "Carry to rig", the one leg the engineer walks. The engineer stands at minus 20 metres beside
the ore chute.

## Palette and rendering

This image is dark. Rock reads as clay #AD7A4F warm brown near the surface, cooling to grey-blue at
depth. Dug space is near black. Machines are steel silhouettes with lit faces.

Interface ground #12141D, wells #1C1F29, edges #333B4D with a #8594AD top bevel. Brass accent #CCA84D.
Text #CCD4E3, dim #8A94A8.

Item inks: clay #AD7A4F · coal #3D404A · iron-bearing ore #6B7A9E · iron ingot #CCD6EB · glimmer
#3DC7D2 · rope #C7A870 · torch #FFC25C.

Machine lamps: starved of fuel #F54233 red, starved of input #F7B838 amber, working #59EB6B green,
idle #858C9E grey.

The game authors its interface on a 640 x 360 grid and presents it at 2x. Keep interface type small and
confident at that scale, with tabular figures.

Show the screen at rest, settled and sharp, with the motion implied by position rather than by blur.

# SINKFORGE — E-menu proposal H: THE CUTAWAY AT SCALE (control)

Generate ONE image, 1920 x 1080, a game interface mockup. Not a collage, not a storyboard.

This is a visual design study for a game menu. The quantities below are a fixture for the drawing.

## What this image is for

This is a control. A previous proposal drew a small factory as a readable engineering cutaway, and it
worked beautifully at six machines. This image asks the same drawing to hold **five hundred machines
over three hundred and eighty metres**, so we can see with our own eyes what that costs.

**Draw it as well as it can possibly be drawn.** Give it every fair advantage: depth compression,
break marks, grouping, whatever a real draughtsman would reach for. The point of the control is to find
out honestly whether a whole-factory cutaway survives scale, so a genuine best attempt is what is
wanted here.

## The game in four lines

Sinkforge is a side-on pixel-art excavation and factory game. A field engineer digs beneath a permanent
surface rig. Gravity carries goods down the passages the player cuts: the hole is a conveyor. Machines
process and lift material, and the rig issues orders for processed goods.

## Layout

A single raised dark panel filling about 80 percent of the screen width and 76 percent of its height,
with the dimmed pixel-art shaft visible around it.

**Left third: the pack.** Two rows of square wells — FREIGHT, five wells with their counts and a
horizontal brass load bar beneath filled to 71 of 90; and KIT, five wells below a hairline with "no
load" beside them. Under the rows, the selected item: Gear Mill, "Iron ingot and ingot to 1 Gear", and
one filled brass button reading **Place in shaft**.

**Right two-thirds: the whole factory, as one cutaway.**

Title: Factory section. Subtitle: Current installation, depths in metres.

Every machine the player owns is on this drawing. Five hundred of them, from the surface rig at 0 down
to the deepest workings at minus 380. The shaft is a dense vertical trunk with lateral galleries
branching off it at many depths. Ore chutes, coal chutes, ingot routes and powered returns thread
between them. A depth ruler runs down the left of the drawing.

Three machines somewhere in this drawing are burning red, and the drawing has to make them findable.
That is the job being tested.

Use whatever the drawing needs to cope: labelled break marks compressing empty spans, grouped blocks
where machines repeat, a legend, varying line weight, whatever helps. Keep every gravity-fed route
descending and keep the powered returns visibly distinct from the falls.

## The fixture

FREIGHT, 71 of 90 carried: Coal 22 · Steel plate 18 · Iron ingot 16 · Copper ore 14 · Glimmer 1
KIT, free: Conduit 12 · Torch 6 · Rope 3 · Plate Press 1 · Gear Mill 1

Gear Mill is the selected well, lit brass. The coal well wears a small red upward triangle, because one
of the red machines in the drawing is out of fuel.

## Palette and rendering

Panel ground #12141D. Modal ground #101218. Slot well #1C1F29. Edges #333B4D with a #8594AD top bevel.
Brass accent #CCA84D. Text #CCD4E3, dim #8A94A8, faint #808A9E.

The cutaway is drawn in the game's own world colours: rock warm clay brown near the surface cooling to
grey-blue at depth, dug space near black, machines as steel silhouettes lit by their own lamps.

Machine lamps: starved of fuel #F54233, starved of input #F7B838, working #59EB6B, idle #858C9E,
no power #5CD6FA.

Item inks: coal #3D404A · steel plate #A8B5CC · iron ingot #CCD6EB · copper ore #E0853D · glimmer
#3DC7D2 · conduit #A8784D · rope #C7A870 · torch #FFC25C.

The game authors its interface on a 640 x 360 grid and presents it at 2x. Keep interface type small and
on-grid. Let the drawing use the space it needs.

Show the screen at rest, settled and sharp.
